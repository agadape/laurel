## 2. Patterns: grand prize vs track prize

**Grand prize patterns**

1. **A new primitive or a whole category, not a feature.** TapeDrive (storage network), CrowdBrain (robot labor market), Unruggable (wallet hardware). The pitch reads as "a new kind of thing exists now", not "X but on chain Y".
2. **Something you can touch, or a real-world bridge.** 2 of the 3 latest Colosseum grand champions are physical (hardware wallet, real robots). ETHGlobal BA finalists include a signing camera and robot rentals. A live physical moment ("the machine moved because a contract said so") is the most memorable demo a human panel sees.
3. **Founder-market fit plus "will they keep building?"** Colosseum names "execution speed, insight, founder-market fit". The NEAR FAQ names sustainability. Regional winners are common: India remittances (Credible, Stablecorp), the Philippine diaspora (KinnectFi), Thai QR parking (ETHPark-QR). **An Indonesia-specific wedge is an asset, not a limitation.**
4. **Bold but legible in one sentence.** "Bet on flight delays", "camera that proves a photo is real", "robots trained by the crowd". Judges repeat the line to each other.
5. **One host primitive used deeply.** NEAR winners (see 01) always had Chain Signatures or TEE at the core. For the grand prize here, the NEAR piece must be **load-bearing**: remove it and the product cannot exist.

**Track prize patterns**

6. **Ride the narrative of the moment, but in a niche.** 2025 tracks were swept by x402/MCP payment infra (Latinum, MCPay, Corbits, zkx402, Payload, Paybot, Hubble). 2026 by prediction/opinion markets (6 of 25 Frontier winners). By Nov 2026 both are crowded. A track win now needs a sharp twist, such as a new resolution mechanism or a new user group.
7. **Privacy consumer apps with emotional stakes reach finals.** Women's health (PrivyCycle), safety (Pace Club), whistleblowing (ZeroLeaks), private QR payments (Zapp, PIVY). The privacy track here wants exactly this. Infra-only privacy is a weaker draw.
8. **New credit and finance primitives win DeFi tracks over yet another DEX.** attn.markets (revenue tokenization), Yumi (onchain underwriting), Senthos (tranches of prediction flow).
9. **Metrics win NEAR AI judges.** Useful Agents (co-run by Avichal Garg's Electric Capital) rewarded hard numbers (Postt "92% approval"). Ship a benchmark table in the README.

**About AI judges (Claude Code, Codex)**

10. NEAR's rules: the AI judges run a **reproducibility check → feature verification → structured report → preliminary score**. Humans decide, and a low AI score alone cannot reject a project. **Only NEAR work committed from 1 Nov is judged.** The README must give "do X → see Y" steps, list the NEAR protocols used, disclose AI-generated code, and avoid marketing (https://nearhackathon.com/HACKATHON.md).
    Devfolio's AI judge write-up (18 May 2026, https://devfolio.co/blog/the-discerning-machine/) makes three points:
    - The main signal is **claims_mismatches**: every pitch claim is checked against the code with file:line citations.
    - A polished README just "becomes a longer list of claims to check".
    - AI judges have a "more consistent floor" than humans.

    → **Under-claim.** Map each claim to a file. Add a `DEMO.md` script plus tests the judge can run, and a `CLAIMS.md` table (claim → file:line → test). Never describe a backend you didn't build.

**Track-specific rules from HACKATHON.md:** thesis is "judged on the evidence"; games is "judged on the game loop, not the token" (bonus if players never notice the chain); memecoins is "judged on the mechanic, not the chart".
